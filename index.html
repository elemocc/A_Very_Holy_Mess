Begin the Quest GitHub Repo

🕯️ Project

From artwork to adventure

A Very Holy Mess starts from Vitale da Bologna’s San Giorgio e il drago. Players become Saint George’s guardian angels and must help him recover and empower his lost weapon before the dragon destroys him.

The project connects physical museum exploration, artwork observation, app-based interaction, storytelling, and tabletop game components.

Main Goals

  • Encourage careful observation of artworks
  • Make religious and historical subjects more accessible
  • Transform the museum visit into an active mission
  • Extend the experience at home through cards and QR codes

🎮 Gameplay

How the experience works

1. Receive a Quest

Saint George asks the player for help and gives the first mission.

2. Explore the Museum

Players move through museum zones and search for the correct artworks.

3. Scan the Artwork

The companion app activates dialogues, clues, animations, and challenges.

4. Complete Challenges

Players solve mini-games based on tracing, tapping, pressing, scrolling, and observing visual details.

5. Progress the Story

Each success unlocks the next quest and brings Saint George closer to victory.

6. Defeat the Dragon

The game is won when players return the empowered weapon to Saint George in time.

Academic and AI statement

Academic Disclaimer

This project was developed as part of a university exam. It is an academic design prototype created for educational purposes and is not an official product, application, or commissioned project of the Pinacoteca Nazionale di Bologna.

Most of the photographs used in this repository were taken by the authors inside the museum with their own camera equipment and are included only as part of the academic prototype and design documentation.

AI Statement

The authors used AI-assisted tools to support the writing process and the generation of images and videos as samples of what the final product could look like.

ChatGPT by OpenAI and Gemini by Google were used for writing support and image processing, while Pippit by ByteDance was used for video generation. All content, design decisions, research findings, game mechanics, and narrative structure are the result of the authors’ own work.
